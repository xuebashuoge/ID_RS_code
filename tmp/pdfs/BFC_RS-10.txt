    c) A few interesting observations.:
  • The amount of transmitted information does not depend on the number of messages to be identified, but only on the error
    probability, which may be taken as a constant. Thus the ordinary transmission rate is effectively zero.
  • If the channel is noisy, a similar argument can be made by using a reliable channel code whose ordinary transmission
    rate approaches zero.
  • The number of identifiable messages depends exponentially on the amount of shared randomness, even though the shared
    randomness R̄ is independent of the message chosen by the transmitter.
  • If the shared randomness must instead be transmitted over a channel, communicating R̄ requires log2 R bits. With a
    channel of capacity C and n channel uses, one may take approximately R = 2nC , leading to a doubly exponential
    number of identifiable messages.
